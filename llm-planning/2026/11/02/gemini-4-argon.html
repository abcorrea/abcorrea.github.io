Evaluating Gemini 4 Argon on End-to-End Planning

Created in November 02, 2026

2026   ·   planning   llms   experiments   ·   llm-planning

Google made Gemini 4 Argon available in the Gemini API in October. I tested it as an end-to-end planner: the model receives a PDDL domain and a PDDL task, and it has to return a plan. This post only reports coverage, i.e., the percentage of solved tasks. I do not discuss time, cost, or plan quality. I compare Argon with Gemini 3.1 Pro in the same setting, and with the LLM-generated heuristics from our NeurIPS 2026 paper.

I used the same setup as in our paper Frontier Large Language Models Rival State-of-the-Art Planners. The prompt contains the domain, the task, and two examples (a Gripper task and a Logistics task, each with its plan). The model has a single attempt and no tools, so it cannot run code or call a planner. Every plan is validated with VAL, and a task counts as solved only if VAL accepts the plan. For Argon, I set the thinking level to high, which is the default for Gemini 3.1 Pro (Argon’s default is medium). Argon ignores temperature and top-P, so I did not set them. Both models used the default output limit of 65,536 tokens.

I used three sets of tasks:

  1. The standard set contains eight domains of the IPC 2023 learning track. We generated 45 new tasks per domain with the competition generators (15 easy, 15 medium, and 15 hard), so the set has 360 tasks.
  2. The obfuscated set has the same 360 tasks, but all names in the domain and task files are replaced by meaningless symbols, such as p1 for a predicate, a1 for an action, and o1 for an object. Comments are removed.
  3. The third set has two new domains that were created during the NeurIPS 2026 rebuttal phase. In cisterns, tanks connected by hoses exchange water one unit at a time, but water only flows from a tank with at least two units more than the destination. In orrery, twisting the coupling between two wheels moves one wheel forward by one mark and the other backward by one mark, and the goal is to bring every wheel back to its reference mark.

The standard and obfuscated sets are the ones from our paper on frontier LLMs. The two new domains were created in June 2026, after Argon’s knowledge cutoff (March 2026, according to its model card).

On the standard set, Argon solves 88% of the tasks and Gemini 3.1 Pro solves 68%. Argon solves almost every task that 3.1 Pro solves. Just two tasks (0.6%) are solved by 3.1 Pro and not by Argon (one in miconic, one in transport). In contrast, 21% of the tasks are solved by Argon and not by 3.1 Pro. The gap grows with the size of the tasks. Both models solve nearly all easy tasks (100% and 99%), while Argon solves 91% of the medium tasks and 74% of the hard tasks, against 72% and 33% for 3.1 Pro.

(Nine of the 15 hard rovers tasks have prompts longer than the 1M-token context window, so neither model can attempt them, and I count them as failures. Neither model solves any hard sokoban task.)

On the obfuscated set, Argon solves 85% of the tasks, three percentage points lower than on the standard set. Gemini 3.1 Pro solves 64%. Moreover, Gemini 3.1 Pro solves no task that Argon fails to solve, and 21% of the tasks are solved by Argon and not by 3.1 Pro. Argon’s coverage on the obfuscated set is also higher than 3.1 Pro’s coverage on the standard set (85% vs. 68%).

How does this compare with LLM-generated heuristics? In our NeurIPS 2026 paper, the LLM writes one heuristic function per domain using small training tasks and refines it with a CEGIS loop, and Pyperplan then uses this heuristic to solve the test tasks.

The CEGIS loop is used to enforce that the heuristic satisfies a given property in the small training tasks. In the NeurIPS 2026 paper, we enforce the heuristics to be direct, i.e., leading directly to a goal through hill-climbing (HC). Its heuristics were written by Gemini 3.1 Pro, and the planner uses them with HC. FF is the classical FF heuristic with greedy best-first search (GBFS).

Over these eight domains, Direct solves 69% of the tasks, about the same as 3.1 Pro end-to-end (68%). Argon end-to-end solves 88%. It has the highest coverage in every domain except miconic, where Direct solves all tasks and Argon solves 98% (in spanner, it ties with 3.1 Pro end-to-end). In sokoban, Direct and 3.1 Pro end-to-end both solve 33% of the tasks, and Argon solves 56%. In rovers, the nine prompts that do not fit in the context window limit end-to-end coverage to at most 80%, while the heuristics do not have this limit.

For cisterns and orrery (the new domains!), these are the results:

Domain Direct (HC) FF (GBFS) Argon, end-to-end
cisterns 30% 34% 91%
orrery 100% 64% 100%

On orrery, Direct and Argon solve every task. On cisterns, Direct solves 30% of the tasks, fewer than FF (34%), and Argon solves 91%.

Two years ago, the motivation to write code instead of doing end-to-end planning was that (1) it was cheaper and (2) it had better performance. It is not obvious that both are still true — although code synthesis is definitely still cheaper than end-to-end.

If open models continue to improve, who knows…
